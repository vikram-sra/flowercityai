# Flower City AI — practical launch plan

Updated 24 September 2026. This supersedes the earlier grants-led plan.

## Purpose and current stage

Help local small businesses use AI with confidence through practical audits, employee education, customer follow-ups and simple automation. The business is registered. Confirm the actual legal entity and registered name before preparing contracts; do not assume it is a sole proprietorship.

The founder can deliver basic automations and practical teaching. Weekly capacity is still to be confirmed. Launch across several local industries using a consistent service and repeatable everyday workflows.

## Customers and positioning

Start with owners in Brampton and Peel who have a repeated administrative task, access to the person doing it, a decision-maker and a budget. Examples include quote follow-ups for trades, common customer replies for shops/restaurants, and meeting recaps for local services.

Positioning: **Practical AI help for the tasks local businesses do every day.**

Local competitors and business-support programmes exist. The differentiator to test is clear service choices, a concrete worked example, patient teaching and client-owned handover. Do not claim market leadership, exclusive expertise or absence of competitors. See the dated review in `docs/launch-review-2026-09-24.md` for research and sources.

A client may start with an audit, training or a tightly scoped workflow. Target audit turnaround: five business days after agreed inputs are received. Implementation, subscriptions, ongoing support, formal certification and grant applications are not included in the audit. The plan must be useful without a follow-on purchase.

Avoid complex voice systems, custom applications and unattended construction pricing until reliable delivery capability or a qualified partner is established.

## Funding model

Sell worthwhile services at their full price. Funding may offset a particular customer's approved eligible costs. Flower City AI is a service provider, not automatically a government grant distributor.

A commercial audit is not automatically a funded DMAP. Provider eligibility and role conflicts matter. The currently linked OCI rules separate the DMAP consultant from the funded technology demonstration vendor for the same customer. TDP is currently marked closed. OJG relaunched in May 2026 but its training/provider exclusions need substantive review. Never turn these programmes into a universal discount.

Maintain the dated funding register in `launch/grant-kit.html`, retain written decisions, and recheck before every funded commitment. No grant award is included in the base revenue forecast.

## Economics and capacity

The earlier $120,000 first-year headline was not supported by its quarterly totals. Use actual capacity and sales evidence instead of a top-down market-share forecast.

Illustration only, valuing owner delivery time at $75/hour:

| Service | Fee | Delivery time | Other direct costs | Contribution after delivery time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Standard audit | $750 | 6 hours | $50 | $250 |
| Training | $1,250 | 8 hours | $75 | $575 |
| Small automation | $2,000 | 14 hours | $100 | $850 |

A $500 pilot audit at eight hours and $50 costs loses $150 on this basis. Keep scope tight and track time. Do not rely on future implementation revenue to make every audit worthwhile.

Four standard audits, two training sessions and one small automation would generate $7,500/month with 54 delivery hours before selling, travel, administration and support. This is a capacity scenario, not a forecast. Deduct insurance, tools, accounting, taxes and other overhead before estimating take-home pay. Confirm weekly availability before committing to this volume.

## Sales and proof

Lead with the owner's task. Use the fictional sample audit to explain the output. A free 15-minute fit call identifies which service, if any, fits the task. Follow with a clear proposal and suitable agreement. Track leads, fit calls, proposals, wins and reasons for declining.

After delivery, collect honest feedback and separate permission for a case study. Publish actual baselines, measurements and limitations. Do not manufacture savings, testimonials, reply speeds or training scores. Measure satisfaction out of ten if useful; do not mislabel that as NPS.

## First-month actions

1. Finalize the audit, training and small-workflow scopes; prepare a service agreement, invoicing and contact details.
2. Verify the Google Form response destination and notifications. Remove unnecessary required fields through the owning account.
3. Hold five useful local conversations and send proposals where justified.
4. Deliver three paid projects across the offer set, logging effort and costs.
5. Offer training directly when that is the right need; deliver at most one small automation if ready.
6. Review actual margins, capacity and the workflow worth repeating.

## Operations and responsibilities

Client-owned accounts, minimal necessary access, MFA, clear scope and a handover are the default. Prefer redacted or fictional information in early work. Agree data handling and retention before using client information in AI tools. Important outgoing messages, quotes, invoices and changes retain human approval until validated.

Confirm banking/bookkeeping, HST obligations with an accountant, suitable service terms, and appropriate insurance. Registration alone does not complete these steps. References and local support contacts are in the dated review.

## Launch measures

Track paid projects by service; actual hours and contribution; clients completing an agreed next step; training task competence; automation reliability and exceptions; time spent selling; cash collected; and consented client feedback. Your first milestone is three useful paid projects across the offer set, not a grant total or a large website.
