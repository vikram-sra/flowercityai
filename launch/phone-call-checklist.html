Discovery Call Checklist
Your 30-minute discovery call

Listen first. Lead with the grant. Close to one next step.

Keep this open during the call and tick as you go. The goal isn't to sell — it's to understand their problem, see if they qualify for funding, and agree on a single, specific next step. Aim to talk 30%, listen 70%.

Before you dial

Re-read their form answer + business name — jot 1–2 notes
Have your calendar, a notepad, and the Product Ladder open
Silence notifications. Stand up, smile — it carries down the line

On the call — the flow

The numbers are the order. Don't rush steps 4–5 — that's where the whole call is won.

1Warm open~30 sec
Say"Hi [name], it's [you] from Flower City AI — thanks for booking. Did I catch you at an OK time?"
2Set the frame — take the pressure off
Say"This is just a no-pressure chat. I want to understand your business and give you a straight read on whether AI can help — even if we never work together. Sound good?"
3Drop the grant hook early
Say"Quick heads-up before we dig in — a lot of what we do can be part-funded by Ontario grants. I'll flag it if you qualify."
4Discover — then be quiet and listenmost of the call
Say"Walk me through a normal day — where does your time actually go?" Then let them talk. Follow their thread, don't jump to solutions.
5Quantify the pain — get a number
Say"Roughly how many hours a week does that eat?" … "And what would you guess that's costing you?"
6Quietly qualify for grants (see checklist below)
Say"Just so I know what funding might apply — you're a for-profit here in Ontario, and under 50 staff, right?"
7Reflect it back + bridge
Say"So if I've got it right, [pain] is costing you about [X] a week. Here's how I'd approach it…"
8Recommend the easy first step (not everything)
Say"I wouldn't start big. The right first move is [AI training / a readiness audit] — low cost, low risk, and it's the piece grant funding attaches to."
9Set honest expectations on the grant
Say"I can't promise approval — funding's limited and first-come. What I can do is build you the strongest case and handle the paperwork."
10Close to ONE specific next step
Say"The natural first step is a short readiness audit. Want me to send a one-page proposal today so you can see the scope and price?"

Discovery questions to keep handy

Ask, then wait. Silence gets you the real answer.

  • What made you reach out now, specifically?
  • What's the one task you wish would just do itself?
  • How are you handling that today?
  • What have you already tried — and what happened?
  • If this were solved tomorrow, what would you do with the time?
  • Who else would be part of a decision like this?
  • What would make this an easy yes for you?

Does this lead qualify for a grant?

Ontario-based and for-profit
Under 50 employees
Willing to put in their share (grants are matched, not free)
Has a real project to fund — adoption or training, not just curiosity

Handling pushback

It's too expensive.
"Fair — that's exactly why we start small and look at grant funding. The first step is often a few hundred dollars, and it pays for itself in hours saved."
Will AI replace my staff?
"No — it takes the boring, repetitive work off their plate so they can do the skilled work you actually hired them for."
I'm not technical at all.
"You don't need to be. I handle the tech and train your team in plain English. If you can use a smartphone, you're fine."
Is the grant thing actually real?
"Yes — Ontario actively funds this. I can't guarantee approval, but I'll tell you honestly if you qualify and handle the paperwork."
I need to think about it.
"Totally reasonable. Can I send a short one-pager and we pencil a quick follow-up for [day]? No pressure either way."

After you hang up

Send the recap + proposal within 24 hours (sooner is better)
Log notes + the agreed next step + date in your tracker
If grant-eligible: note the program and start their DMAP
Book the follow-up before it goes cold

Golden rules

✓ Do

  • Listen ~70%, talk ~30%
  • Use their exact words back to them
  • Lead with the problem and the grant
  • End on one specific next step
  • Be honest when they won't qualify

✕ Don't

  • Pitch a deployment you can't deliver solo yet
  • Guarantee grant approval or a dollar amount
  • Use jargon — say it like you'd say it to a friend
  • Wing the price — anchor to your Product Ladder
  • Leave without a scheduled next action
