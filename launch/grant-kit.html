Part 2 · The Play — internal

How the funding actually works — and how to run it clean.

The mechanics behind the pitch, the current 2026 program landscape, your positioning, and the guardrails that keep you honest and out of trouble.

The mechanic: DMAP → Technology Demonstration

  • The funder doesn't pay you directly. It funds the business, which then engages vendors (that's you) to do the work.
  • DMAP first. The Digital Modernization & Adoption Plan is the entry step — a roadmap of what to adopt and why. You can be the one who prepares it. This is a natural paid entry product (your AI Readiness Audit, repackaged).
  • Then the Technology Demonstration. Larger implementation funding (recent programs up to ~$100K, typically around 50% matched) is awarded against that plan. You deliver the funded build.
  • Your two revenue moments: (1) the plan/audit, (2) the funded implementation — plus a retainer after.

2026 program landscape — verify before every pitch

Use this
Ontario Centre of Innovation — DCC / DMAP / TDP

Your primary lever. Actively funded and expanded for AI/digital adoption. Ontario for-profit, 1–50 employees, first-come-first-served while funds last. Confirm current intake at oc-innovation.ca.

Gone
CDAP (federal $15K / $100K loan)

Closed March 2024, no federal replacement as of 2026. Do not reference it or build a pitch on it.

Paused
Canada-Ontario Job Grant (COJG)

Under ministerial review / paused in 2026. Great for funding AI training when active — but don't promise it until intake reopens. Check ontario.ca/cojg.

Your books
SR&ED + NRC IRAP (federal)

For R&D and building custom tech — relevant to Flower City AI's own taxes if you build proprietary IP, not for a client buying off-the-shelf tools. Don't offer clients SR&ED for tool adoption.

Position yourself as the implementation partner

  • You're not a grant-writing shop; you're the AI vendor who also makes the funding easy.
  • Bundle it: audit → DMAP → funded build → retainer. The grant de-risks the biggest cheque.
  • Grant help is your differentiator vs. freelancers — none of them do this.
  • Become known to your local OCI advisor / Brampton Board of Trade as the AI implementer.

Guardrails — do not cross these

  • Never guarantee funding or a specific dollar amount. Say “potential” and “if eligible.”
  • Never imply government endorsement of Flower City AI. You're a paid vendor, full stop.
  • Verify current intake before you cite a program — windows open and close.
  • Don't misrepresent off-the-shelf tools as R&D to fit a grant. Frame projects honestly around real modernization.
  • Disclose your role if you help prepare an application — you're the implementer, not an impartial advisor.

Where to point people

  • OCI programs: oc-innovation.ca — confirm DCC/DMAP/TDP intake & eligibility
  • COJG status: ontario.ca/cojg or 1-800-387-5656
  • Business registration: ontario.ca — your own $60 first step
  • Local network: Brampton Board of Trade — get known as the local AI implementer
